Money Foundations

What Is Personal Finance?

Personal finance is the way you manage your money and make financial decisions. It includes how you earn, spend, save, invest, and plan for your future.

Remember: Good financial management starts with understanding the money you already have.

Income, Expenses & Savings

Income is the money you receive. Expenses are the costs you pay. Savings are the money you intentionally set aside for future needs or goals. Understanding all three gives you a clearer picture of where your money is going.

Needs vs Wants

A need is something essential to your wellbeing or responsibilities, while a want is something you would like to have but can live without. The difference can depend on your circumstances, so the goal is not to avoid spending on things you enjoy, but to make the distinction before making a decision.

A useful question: Do I need this, or do I simply want it right now?

Understanding Net Worth

Net worth gives you a simple picture of your overall financial position. It is the value of what you own, known as assets, minus what you owe, known as liabilities.

Remember: Income shows what you earn. Net worth helps show what you have built.
02

Budgeting & Spending

What Is a Budget?

A budget is a plan for how you intend to use your money. It helps you decide what should be spent, saved, or set aside for specific goals before the money disappears.

Tracking Your Spending

Tracking your spending helps you see where your money actually goes rather than relying on memory. Even small purchases can add up over time, so knowing your spending patterns can help you make better decisions.

Fixed vs Variable Expenses

Fixed expenses generally stay similar from month to month, while variable expenses can change. Knowing the difference makes it easier to understand which parts of your spending are predictable and which require more attention.

Managing Impulse Spending

Impulse spending happens when you make a purchase without properly considering whether it fits your priorities. Giving yourself time to think before buying can help separate a genuine need or planned purchase from a temporary desire.

03

Saving

What Is Saving?

Saving means setting money aside instead of spending it immediately. Savings can help you prepare for future expenses, unexpected situations, and goals that matter to you.

Saving vs Investing

Saving generally focuses on keeping money available for future needs and shorter-term goals. Investing involves putting money into assets with the expectation of potential growth over time, while accepting a greater level of risk.

Emergency Funds

An emergency fund is money set aside for unexpected expenses or financial disruptions. Its purpose is to give you a financial buffer so that an unexpected event does not immediately become a crisis.

Setting Savings Goals

A savings goal gives your money a purpose. Instead of simply saying you want to save more, define what you are saving for, how much you need, and when you would like to reach the goal.

04

Investing

What Is Investing?

Investing means putting money into an asset with the expectation that it may grow or generate income over time. Unlike ordinary saving, investing involves the possibility of losing some or all of the money you put in.

Long-Term vs Short-Term Investing

Short-term investing focuses on a relatively near-term objective, while long-term investing gives an investment more time to potentially grow. The appropriate approach depends on your goals, timeframe, and ability to handle changes in value.

Risk & Diversification

Every investment carries some level of risk. Diversification means spreading your money across different investments rather than relying entirely on one. It can help reduce the impact of a poor result from a single investment, although it cannot remove investment risk completely.

Common Beginner Mistakes

Beginners can sometimes invest without understanding what they are buying, follow trends without doing their own research, or expect quick and guaranteed returns. Taking time to understand an investment and its risks is an important first step.

05

Financial Decisions

Debt vs Saving

Managing debt and building savings are both important parts of financial planning. The right balance depends on factors such as the type and cost of the debt, your income, and your financial priorities.

Should You Invest All Your Extra Money?

Having extra money does not automatically mean it should all be invested. Before investing, consider whether you have upcoming needs, financial goals, an emergency buffer, and enough flexibility to handle unexpected expenses.

Lifestyle Inflation

Lifestyle inflation happens when your spending increases as your income increases. Earning more can improve your life, but if every increase in income immediately becomes additional spending, your financial progress may remain limited.

Making Better Money Decisions

Good financial decisions are rarely about finding one perfect rule. They involve understanding your situation, considering the consequences, and choosing an option that fits your goals, priorities, and circumstances.
